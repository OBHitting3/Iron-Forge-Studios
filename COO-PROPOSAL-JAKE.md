## 3. The Equity: What You Get

### The Split: 70/30

| Factor | What It Means |
|--------|---------------|
| I originated every project | Founder premium |
| I built all 8 projects before you join | Sweat equity already invested |
| All existing code, architecture, research is mine | IP contribution |
| You're joining pre-revenue but post-MVP | Real risk, but products exist |
| You bring business/numbers skills I don't have | Critical gap filled |
| No salary for either of us initially | Equal sacrifice going forward |

### Why 70/30

| Scenario | Typical Split | Source |
|----------|--------------|-------|
| Both start from scratch together, Day 1 | 50/50 or 55/45 | Cleverism; Promise Legal |
| **Technical founder with shipped MVPs + business partner joining pre-revenue** | **65/35 to 75/25** | **Lexology; Hunt Club; ICanPitch** |
| Founder hiring COO as employee post-revenue | 2–5% (options) | TopStartups; Wellfound |

70/30 is the sweet spot for our situation:
1. I've already built the products — that's real value that exists today
2. But you're taking real risk joining pre-revenue with no salary
3. 30% is serious co-founder equity — you're a real partner, not an employee
4. It's more than the 20–25% typical for this scenario because I want you fully bought in
5. We both eat what we kill — no salary until revenue supports it

### Vesting: How We Protect Each Other

| | OBHitting3 (70%) | Jake (30%) |
|---|---|---|
| Vesting period | 4 years | 4 years |
| Cliff | 1 year | 1 year |
| At 12-month cliff | 17.5% vested | 7.5% vested |
| Monthly after cliff | +1.46%/month | +0.625%/month |
| Fully vested (48 months) | 70% | 30% |

**Why vesting matters (even between friends):**
- If either of us walks away after 2 months, we don't keep equity we didn't earn
- After 12 months, you've proven commitment — 7.5% is yours no matter what
- After 48 months, the full 30% is locked in permanently
- This is standard for **every startup partnership** — it protects the company and the friendship
- 50% of startups fail due to co-founder conflict. A vesting schedule prevents the worst outcomes

## 6. What We Put in Writing

### Non-Negotiable Protections (For Both of Us)

| Protection | What It Does |
|------------|-------------|
| **4-year vesting, 1-year cliff** | Neither of us walks away with equity we didn't earn |
| **Co-Founder agreement** | Defines roles, equity, decision-making, dispute resolution |
| **IP assignment** | All existing code is assigned to the company (not personal) |
| **Decision-making** | CEO has final say on product/technical; COO has final say on operations/business; major decisions (>$5K spend, new hires, equity changes) require both |
| **Exit provisions** | What happens if one of us wants out — buyback terms, right of first refusal |
| **Non-compete** | Neither of us builds competing products while active |
| **Dissolution clause** | If we shut down, how assets and IP are divided |

### What We Should Both Ask a Lawyer About

- LLC vs. S-Corp (tax implications at our revenue level)
- Operating agreement / Co-Founder agreement template
- Vesting schedule documentation
- IP assignment for existing code
- Whether to do a formal valuation before equity grant
- How to structure profit distributions vs. reinvestment
